# Imagery & Composition Evidence Memo — Langtang Lirung Collapse, 26 August 2026

**Compiled:** 3 September 2026 (all statements current as of this date)
**Scope:** Published open-web evidence bearing on the lubrication question — was the initiating mass mobile because (a) it was itself ice-rich, (b) it entrained the glacier below the scar, or (c) monsoon water in the valley did the work?
**Method notes:** Every claim is date-stamped and tagged **[M]** measurement, **[I]** interpretation, or **[S]** speculation. Single-source claims are flagged. "No published evidence yet" is stated explicitly where true.

---

## 1. THE SCAR

**Location.** North flank/face of Langtang Lirung (7,227 m), inside Nepali territory, above the Chhochen Khola, which drains to the Lende (Lhende) Khola, thence to the Bhote Koshi at Rasuwagadhi. Coordinates cited: ~28.2765° N, 85.5194° E (Dan Shugar's Planet 25 vs 26 Aug comparison, via Geoscopy 29 Aug) **[M]**; 28.28532° N, 85.52515° E (Bethan Davies, AntarcticGlaciers.org, 27 Aug) **[M]**. These differ by ~1 km; both are early visual placements.

**Elevation band of failure.** Reported values scatter:
- 5,200 m (Kathmandu Post 28 Aug, citing satellite analysis) **[M/I]**
- 5,200–5,400 m (Wikipedia consolidation, citing Shugar) **[M/I]**
- ~5,600 m (geopera reconstruction, 28 Aug) **[I]**
- ~5,800 m (geopera predictability post, 31 Aug — their own source-zone delineation) **[I]**
- 5,100 m top, running to ~3,000 m (Jakob Steiner via Geoscopy, 29 Aug) **[I]**
- ~5,000 m (Nepali Times, 28 Aug) **[I]**

**Fall height.** Most sources: 1,000–1,200 m vertical into the valley (Petley 26 Aug; Copernicus EMS; Shugar; Kathmandu Post). Outliers: 1,400 m (Basanta Raj Adhikari via Down To Earth, 30 Aug), 1,500 m (Nepali Times drone article, 29 Aug), ">2 km" (ABC News 28 Aug — likely conflating total path). The Steiner framing (5,100→3,000 m) gives ~2,100 m to the damming point.

**Dimensions.**
- Width ~600 m: Shugar ("a piece of glacier roughly 600 metres wide"), repeated by geopera (28 Aug) and Kathmandu Post (28 Aug: "about 600 metres wide — of glacier and rock... from an altitude of 5,200 metres, falling 1,200 metres"). **[M]** (multi-source, from Planet/SkySat imagery)
- Detached area ~0.2 km² (Shugar, via Wikipedia). **[M]** single-attribution.
- **Outlier:** Nepali Times (28 Aug): "a clean cleavage about 1.5 km wide in a hanging glacier." **[I]** SINGLE SOURCE, unattributed, 2.5× the consensus width. Treat with caution.

**Rock vs ice content of the scar — qualitative only.** Convergent interpretation from independent analysts that this was a **bedrock failure that carried a hanging glacier (or lower glacier tongue) with it**, i.e., the source mass contained both rock and glacier ice:
- Shugar (phys.org/AP, 28 Aug): "You have this big bedrock failure that took part of the glacier with it." **[I]** from Planet + later clearer imagery.
- GFZ (via Geoscopy, 29 Aug): "break-off of a hanging glacier and underlying bedrock." **[I]**
- Steiner (The Conversation-adjacent SciAm piece, 27 Aug): "You basically had the lower part of a glacier tongue that sheared off because the rock below failed." **[I]**
- Steiner (AP/PBS, 27 Aug): "it's like someone cut with a huge knife through the whole lower part and it fell off." **[I]**
- Drone footage interpretation (Nepali Times, 29 Aug): "the rock face bearing a hanging glacier at the end of an ice field below the summit of Langtang Lirung collapsed... the bedrock itself crumbled taking the ice above it down." Explicitly framed as contradicting "earlier theories that it was an ice mass on the glacier that failed." **[I]** from Chinese radar-station drone video.
- Mohd. Farooq Azam, ICIMOD (Kathmandu Post, 28 Aug): "bedrock failure" with glacier swept along. **[I]**
- USGS (via Geoscopy, 29 Aug): declined to choose between "a landslide incorporating part of a glacier or a glacial collapse." **[I]** — the formal agency position was agnostic as of 29 Aug.
- Shirzaei (Nature news, 2 Sept): the moving mass pre-failure was a "glacier–rock system" — both together. **[M/I]** from Sentinel-1 InSAR.

**No quantitative ice fraction of the source mass has been published anywhere as of 3 Sept 2026.**

**Failure structure/timing.** USGS: seismic signal initially reported as M4.4 tectonic, revised to M~5.2 "generated by the collapse itself" **[M]**. Wikipedia also cites GFZ recording Mw 5.7 — discrepant magnitude, single-source, unresolved. geopera seismology post (1 Sept): "five distinct pulses inside that spike, consistent with a staged, domino-style failure" **[M]** (waveform) + **[I]** (staging); their predictability post reads the staging as "consistent with a mass coming apart wet" **[I]** — directly relevant to the lubrication question. Border CCTV time 08:44 NPT; seismic origin ~08:37 NPT.

**Precursors — CONFLICTING EVIDENCE, flag prominently:**
- Shirzaei/Virginia Tech (Nature news, 2 Sept; Gizmodo 2 Sept): Sentinel-1 InSAR 8 Jan–18 Aug 2026 shows the glacier–rock system creeping at ~10 mm/month near the collapse zone, with **acceleration** in the lead-up: "The acceleration is more important than the velocity." **[M/I]**
- geopera predictability post (31 Aug): the source zone's radar coherence is "saturated at zero" at 12–24-day intervals (too chaotic for coherent InSAR), offset tracking shows "under two metres of total movement in a hundred days" with "**no acceleration in any window**," including the final 48 hours; ICESat-2 shows only ~1.3 m of thinning over two years ("ordinary glacier ablation"); conclusion: the failure was "brittle" and "did not telegraph." **[M/I]**
- These two open analyses of the same mountain reach opposite conclusions about precursory motion. Neither is peer-reviewed. Do not treat "precursory creep" as settled.

## 2. THE GLACIER BELOW THE SCAR — the key discriminator

**Bottom line: as of 3 September 2026, there is NO published post-event measurement of the glacier surface below the failed face. None. The route-(b) fingerprint (gouged/lowered glacier along the runout) has neither been confirmed nor excluded by any open source.**

What exists, and why the gap persists:

1. **Naming/identification.** No published source names the glacier under the north-face scar. "Lirung Glacier" in the literature is the debris-covered tongue in the **south-west cirque** above Kyanjin Gomba (Langtang Valley side) — it is NOT the glacier under this scar and appears in coverage only as regional context (Geoscopy, 29 Aug, lists Lirung/Shalbachum/Langtang/Langshisha tongues as stagnant since 2003, "moving under 10 metres a year"). The ice below the north-face failure — an "ice field below the summit" per the drone-footage description — is unnamed in all coverage reviewed. Any internal memo calling it "Lirung Glacier" should be corrected to "unnamed north-face glacier/ice field above the Chhochen Khola."

2. **Optical imagery of the runout's upper reach is cloud-blocked.** geopera (28 Aug): Sentinel-2 "largely unsuccessful due to cloud cover." geopera stereo post (1 Sept): the 27 Aug WorldView-3 stereo DEM covers only ~45% of the valley floor, and the post contains **no glacier data at all** — their measured erosion/deposition starts in the gorge reaches well below the source. ESA's Sentinel-2 pair (12 vs 27 Aug) shows river-corridor change, not the source glacier. Timure/upper areas sit "almost entirely under the cloud mask."

3. **Interpretive narrative exists but is not evidence.** The claim that the mass "fell onto the flatter glacier below" where "friction melted the ice":
   - Nepali Times (28 Aug): "The bedrock fracture then sent the rock and ice tumbling down at least 1,000m onto the flatter glacier below. This kinetic energy... and the resulting friction melted the ice." **[I]/[S]** — UNATTRIBUTED ("Scientists say"-style sourcing throughout; confirmed on re-read that no named scientist or institution stands behind this sentence).
   - Sunwi Maskey, ICIMOD (Kathmandu Post, 28 Aug): friction melted glacier ice "into water as it travelled downhill. The mass also swept soil, rocks and sediments." **[I]** — ambiguous between melting of ice carried from the source and melting of ice encountered en route.
   - Ghosh & Tielidze (The Conversation, 1 Sept): the fall "would likely have melted the ice in the avalanche **and any in the valley**." **[S]** — explicitly conditional ("would likely"); this is the only sentence found that even gestures at valley ice being incorporated, and it is speculation.
   - Poul Christoffersen (ABC, 28 Aug): "The friction and heat that are associated with this avalanche as it travels down" (melt framing). **[I]**
   None of these rests on observed surface change of the glacier below.

4. **The one observational crumb:** NESRA FloodWatch (2 Sept): "Glaciers and landslide deposits around the source zone are reported unstable" — unquantified, secondhand, and about future hazard rather than what the avalanche did to the glacier surface. **[S]** SINGLE SOURCE.

5. **Possible indirect hint, unconnected by any author:** The Conversation (1 Sept) reports "a small lake has formed within the **newly created depression** in the valley" near the source. A fresh closed depression high in the runout could be an excavation signature (or simply deposit damming) — but no published source interprets it, locates it precisely, or ties it to glacier excavation. **[I]** SINGLE SOURCE phrasing; NESRA's "crater lake... under monitoring" (2 Sept) may be the same feature.

**What would settle it and hasn't appeared:** pre/post DEM differencing over the north-face glacier and Chhochen Khola headwater (Pléiades/WorldView stereo, or the geopera stereo extended upvalley on a clearer pass), or ICIMOD/Chinese drone photogrammetry of the impact zone. Chinese drones overflew the avalanche site on 27 Aug (Nepali Times) but only lake observations from those flights have reached print.

## 3. THE DEPOSIT

**Upper valley (Chhochen/Lende headwater, 0–5 km from source):**
- geopera (28 Aug, from PlanetScope/SkySat): a sediment-laden pond ~0.06 km² (~240 × 490 m) at 2.4 km from the source; impounded volume "of order 0.5 to 1.5 million cubic metres." **[M]** (area) / **[I]** (volume).
- Chinese drone inspections (via Nepali Times, 28 Aug): "a lake has formed along the Lhende Khola... impounded by the falling debris." **[M]**
- No thickness, ice-content, or melt-out observations of the proximal deposit have been published. **Nobody has described the proximal deposit as ice-rich OR ice-poor. The route-(a)-vs-(c) deposit fingerprint is unmeasured in open sources.**

**The two barrier lakes:**
- **Lower lake** (Chhochen Khola / Purepu Tsangpo confluence per Wikipedia; ~11 km NE of the Nepal–Tibet border per Geoscopy): ~2,000,000 m³ (Chinese authorities, 27 Aug) **[M]**, with a further ~3,000,000 m³ of inflow forecast over three days (Chinese statement via ABC, 28 Aug) **[I]**. Fate: Geoscopy says breached 28 Aug with only ~0.6 m downstream rise; Wikipedia says it "slowly drained out and was largely empty by 30 August"; a Chinese technical statement of 30 Aug (via NESRA) called it fully drained. Minor internal inconsistency (breach vs slow drain), no flood resulted either way.
- **Upper lake** (high, near the collapse zone — plausibly geopera's 2.4 km pond and/or The Conversation's "newly created depression" lake): Jeffrey Kargel (Planetary Science Institute, via Independent/Yahoo, 30 Aug): footage "extremely concerning... if the upper lake goes, its water would pour into the dammed lake below and the two could burst out together" **[S]** — but assessed unlikely to produce major flooding by 29 Aug (Geoscopy). NESRA (2 Sept): still under monitoring; "further collapse could create new impoundments."

**Gorge and mid-valley (measured, geopera stereo DEM, WorldView-3 27 Aug, published 1 Sept — the best quantitative deposit data yet):**
- Upper gorge (Rasuwagadhi reach): valley floor **8–12 m LOWER** than pre-event — net erosion, not deposition. **[M]**
- Confined sections above Syabrubesi: 2–5 m erosion; incision 13–21 m in places downstream. **[M]**
- Totals in the ~45% of the corridor visible: **3.2 M m³ erosion vs 0.9 M m³ deposition** — the mapped reach was net erosional by ~3.5×. **[M]**
- Main depositional body: a debris sheet ~3 km long, **12–18 m thick in its core**, beginning ~km 40.5 where the valley opens (Syabrubesi area). **[M]**
- **Retraction:** geopera's 28 Aug parallax-based claim of a "12 million cubic metre wedge" of new valley floor at km 33–38 was withdrawn on 1 Sept: "It was noise." Use only the stereo numbers.
- Boulder census: 4,329 clasts >1 m across 37 ha; largest block 26 m; competence velocities 19–26 m/s. **[M]**
- Trimlines: median ~70 m through the gorges; 40–134 m at the border. Velocities 37 m/s (Lende Khola), 45–52 m/s (border), ~50 m/s (gorge), dropping to 11 m/s at Syabrubesi. **[M/I]** (geopera, 28 Aug)
- Flow character: geopera's calibrated 2-D flood-sediment model (1 Sept): "this flood moved like water, not like a viscous debris slurry"; sediment "behaves finer than we had assumed" (critical shear stress 250→150 Pa, settling velocity 0.15→0.054 m/s, Manning n 0.050→0.034; modeled deposition fell from 21 to ~5 M m³ after calibration). **[M-model]** — the downstream flow was water-dominated, whatever lubricated the initial mass.
- No melt-out features, kettle holes, or ice-cored deposit descriptions appear in any source. **[explicit gap]**

**Duration:** seismic tremor plateau ~3 hours after the collapse spike (geopera seismology, 1 Sept) **[M]**; an unexplained discrete burst 3 h 08 m after collapse. Galchhi gauge: +9 m in 30 min; Malekhu +7 m (ICIMOD, 26 Aug) **[M]**.

Tension worth noting: the attributed source estimates (100–200 M m³) sit 1–2 orders of magnitude above both the EGU preliminary (0.5–10 M m³) and the measured valley-floor change (~4 M m³ in the visible 45%). A 100–200 M m³ source with only ~1 M m³ of measured deposition in the mapped corridor implies either (i) most mass stayed high in the (unmapped, cloud-covered) upper valley, (ii) most "volume" was ice that melted and left as water, or (iii) the big estimates are inflated. All three readings are live; no published source has confronted the discrepancy.

## 5. VERDICT ROW — what open evidence says about each lubrication route

| Route | Fingerprint | Verdict (3 Sept 2026) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Source mass itself ice-rich** (hanging glacier fell with the rock) | Ice visible in/on detached mass; ice-bearing scar | **SUPPORTED (qualitatively; unquantified)** | Convergent independent interpretation — Shugar (Planet imagery), GFZ, Steiner, Azam/ICIMOD, and the drone footage (leading *white* stream descending before the brown dust cloud) — all describe a bedrock failure that took a hanging glacier / lower glacier tongue with it. Shirzaei's InSAR treats the pre-failure mass as a coupled "glacier–rock system." No source disputes that substantial glacier ice was in the initiating mass. **But no ice fraction has been published**, so "ice-rich enough to explain slurry behaviour" remains open. |
| **(b) Entrainment/excavation of the glacier below the scar** | Gouged/lowered glacier surface along upper runout | **SILENT — no published evidence for or against** | No post-event measurement of the glacier below the failed face exists in any open source. The upper runout was cloud-covered in Sentinel-2 and in the 27 Aug WorldView-3 stereo (which covers ~45% of the corridor, none of it glacier). The oft-repeated "fell onto the flatter glacier below and friction melted the ice" line is unattributed journalism (Nepali Times 28 Aug) echoed conditionally by The Conversation ("would likely have melted... any in the valley" — speculation). The glacier below isn't even named in coverage (it is NOT the SW-side Lirung Glacier). One indirect crumb: a "newly created depression" hosting a new lake near the source (Conversation, 1 Sept; possibly NESRA's "crater lake") — consistent with excavation but interpreted by no one. This discriminator is genuinely untested; the physics-model inference is ahead of the published imagery here. |
| **(c) Monsoon water already in the valley** | Neither (a) nor (b); relatively ice-poor deposit | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED as a contributor; the discriminating deposit observation is unpublished** | Petley: "no evidence that this was a GLOF." GFZ/Geoscopy list river flow and "water embedded in the sediments entrained en route" among water sources. geopera's calibration finds the downstream flood "moved like water, not like a viscous debris slurry," and their seismically staged failure was "consistent with a mass coming apart wet" — late-monsoon water in the source mass at failure (44 days above freezing at the source zone mid-June–collapse). None of this addresses the *initial* kilometre of motion, and **no source describes the proximal deposit's ice content** (ice-poor would favour (c), ice-rich (a)). Deposit composition: no published evidence yet. |

**Net reading:** (a) is the only route with positive published support, but only qualitatively — everyone agrees ice fell with the rock; nobody has said how much. (b) is untested, not refuted — the fingerprint area is unmeasured and even unnamed in coverage; the popular "melted on the glacier below" narrative is unattributed interpretation that *assumes* something like (b)/(a) without evidence. (c) has downstream support (water-like flow, wet staged failure, monsoon timing) but the proximal deposit observation that would separate it from (a) does not exist yet. A clear-sky stereo pass or drone photogrammetry over the north-face glacier and proximal deposit would discriminate; none has been published.
