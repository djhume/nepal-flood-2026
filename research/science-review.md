# Literature Review: Water Sources in Rock/Ice-Avalanche Floods & Downstream Wave Evolution

Compiled 2 Sept 2026 (research agent sweep). Supports testing the hypothesis that the Aug 2026
Langtang→Trishuli flood water was mostly channel/river-derived rather than frictional ice melt.

## 1. Chamoli 2021 — the crux case (melt DID dominate there)

**Shugar et al. 2021, Science 373:300–306** ([doi](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abh4455), [open-access PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/175202/1/Shugar%20et%20al%20Uttarakhand%20FINAL%20Maintext%20wFigs.pdf)) — Kargel co-author:

- 26.9 Mm³ (95% CI 26.5–27.3) wedge failure off Ronti Peak, ~5,500→3,700 m in ~1 min; **~80% rock / 20% ice by volume** (~5–6 Mm³ ice); total drop ~3,400 m; H/L = 0.16 at Tapovan (26 km).
- Video-derived hydraulics: frontal velocity 25 m/s (Rishiganga HPP, 15 km) → 16 → 12 m/s (Tapovan); discharge ~8,200–14,200 m³/s at Rishiganga → 2,900–4,900 below Tapovan; duration 10–20 min at Rishiganga.
- **Water source conclusion:** most of the 5–6 Mm³ of ice warmed from −8 °C and **melted through frictional heating** during the 3,400 m descent. They explicitly bounded and rejected alternatives: no GLOF, no lake, storm precip only 4–7% of the ice's water equivalent; river water, groundwater, snow, wet sediment, HPP reservoir "even taken together (with generous error margins)… a small amount."
- The 80:20 ratio was "**almost exactly the critical value required for near-complete melting of the ice**" — i.e., ~80% of ALL dissipated PE had to enter the ice. Named severity drivers: extraordinary fall height, worst-case rock:ice ratio, infrastructure in the path.
- Their r.avaflow runs showed the avalanche had to shed ~8 Mm³ of solids at the Ronti Gad–Rishiganga confluence for a fluid-rich front to match observed discharges; simulated travel times within 5% of seismic/video.

**Caveats for transfer to Trishuli 2026:** Chamoli was **7 February** — winter low flow (tens of m³/s). The "river water is minor" finding is conditional on that; it is not a general law. Early ICIMOD analysis ([link](https://www.icimod.org/article/understanding-the-chamoli-flood-cause-process-impacts-and-context-of-rapid-infrastructure-development/)) had a more mixed taxonomy (PE could melt only ~2.7 Mm³; invoked old avalanche-deposit ice, river water "pushed ahead", saturated sediments) — superseded but a useful candidate-source checklist.

**Follow-ups:** Munch et al. 2024 JGR-ES thermomechanical 4-material model (rock/ice/snow/water) with frictional heating and phase change ([doi](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024JF007805)); open multi-phase thermo-mechanical model ([arXiv:2404.06130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06130)) — uses shear-heat-to-ice-melt fractions **0.3–0.7** (tuned, not measured), μ_rock 0.8, μ_ice 0.4.

## 2. Frictional melting energetics

- PE mgh → heat; melting ice takes L ≈ 334 kJ/kg (+ ~2.0 kJ/kg·K sensible). 1 kg dropping 1,000 m releases 9.81 kJ ⇒ **pure ice falling 1 km can melt at most ~3% of its own mass**. Rock subsidizes melt per kg of ice.
- Chamoli check: ice mass fraction ≈ 7.7%; warming+melting it needs ~27 kJ per kg of mixture vs ~33 kJ/kg available from 3,400 m ⇒ ~80% of heat into ice ⇒ "critical ratio."
- Langtang 2026: initial fall ~1,200 m ⇒ ~12 kJ/kg — under half the Chamoli-like requirement even at perfect transfer. **Energetics cap melt water for this event well below the Chamoli benchmark**, before heat-partition realism (0.3–0.7) is applied.
- Reference cases: **Kolka-Karmadon 2002** (~100 Mm³, mostly ice, 20 km at 50–80 m/s; mobility from basal fluidization + continuous frictional melt; Huggel et al. 2005 [NHESS](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/5/173/2005/)) — the ice-rich end member, melt supply effectively unlimited. **Huascarán 1970** (Evans et al. 2009 [ESG](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0013795209001574)): revised down to ~7.5 Mm³, fluidity attributed to **entrainment of snow/meltwater from Glacier 511** — even the classic case is substantially an entrainment story. Bessette-Kirton's Alaska work on rock avalanches onto glaciers ([Landslides 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10346-017-0879-7), [JGR 2018 methods](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2017JF004512)).

## 3. Entrainment and bulking

- **Iverson & Ouyang 2015** Rev. Geophys. — standard entrainment theory (boundary jump conditions).
- **Iverson et al. 2011, Nature Geoscience 4:116–121** ([link](https://www.nature.com/articles/ngeo1040)): flume debris flows over **wet** bed sediment develop high basal pore pressures, liquefy the bed — "mass and momentum of a flow that liquefies its bed can grow explosively." Dry beds retard flows. **Monsoon-saturated Himalayan channels are the wet-bed case.**
- **Sikkim 2023 GLOF** ([Science 2025](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads2659)): ~50 Mm³ lake water eroded ~270 Mm³ sediment (5.4:1); at porosity 0.2–0.35 the entrained fill also liberates tens of Mm³ of pore water — same order as the lake release.
- **Seti River 2012 — Kargel's own precedent for river-water dominance:** ~22 Mm³ rockfall off Annapurna IV, >3,000 m drop. Kargel et al. concluded flood water was dominated by **river/snowmelt water impounded behind rockslide dams built over preceding weeks** ("several million m³"), released as ~27 surges; melt could account for "roughly a tenth of the falling snow and ice" ([SANDRP summary](https://sandrp.in/2014/01/26/explained-seti-river-floods-in-may-2012-nepal-a-chain-of-events-starting-at-25000-feet/), [EGU 2014 abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014EGUGA..1612448K/abstract)). Peak-discharge estimates span 1,000–12,300 m³/s across studies; best-fit hydrodynamic reconstruction ~3,700 m³/s at Kharapani, 23 km in ~30 min ([NHESS 2022](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/22/3105/2022/)).
- **Melamchi 2021:** ordinary monsoon water (>200 mm rain/week) + sequential temporary-dam breaches (670,000 m³ Melamchigaon dam; 104,000 m³ Larcha dam) + ~13 Mm³ sediment deposited — deadly through bulking, not an exotic water source ([ICIMOD](https://www.icimod.org/article/the-melamchi-flood-disaster/)).
- **Costa & Schuster 1988** (GSA Bull. 100): of 73 landslide dams, 27% failed within 1 day, 85% within a year, >90% by overtopping.

## 4. Water standing in a monsoon Himalayan channel

- Trishuli at Betrawati (DHM stn 447): mean annual ~231 m³/s; dry ~50; wet-season avg ~650; Jul–Aug monthly peaks to ~1,000 m³/s; ~70% of annual flow in monsoon ([flow-frequency curves](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Flow-frequency-curves-of-daily-discharge-data-from-the-Trishuli-at-Betrawati-station-no_fig5_344340890)).
- Rasuwagadhi HEP design Q 80 m³/s at Q40; UT-3A/3B design 51 m³/s.
- With monsoon Q ≈ 300–600 m³/s and v ≈ 2–4 m/s, A = Q/v ≈ 100–300 m² ⇒ **~0.1–0.3 Mm³ standing water per km ⇒ ~10–30 Mm³ per 100 km**, plus inflow ~1.1–2.2 Mm³/h. (A wave doesn't scoop all of it into its peak — it overruns, mixes, displaces — but Pierson & Scott show ambient river water progressively dominating such flows.)

## 5. Downstream pulse evolution

- **Kinematic wave theory** (Lighthill & Whitham 1955): c = dQ/dA rises with stage ⇒ **front steepens toward a bore, tail stretches, hydrograph broadens, peak attenuates** unless re-fed. Steep channels spawn roll waves / multiple surges (Zanuttigh & Lamberti 2007); debris-flow surges carry high-resistance bouldery fronts pushed by a liquefied body (Iverson 1997). Seti 2012 arrived as ~27 waves; the 2016 Bhote Koshi GLOF as two seismically distinct pulses — water first, bedload seconds later ([phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2018-10-ground-devastating-insights.html)).
- **Observed attenuation:** Dig Tsho 1985: 2,350 m³/s at 7.1 km → 1,375 at 27 km (~40% loss/20 km); GLOF peaks 7–60× seasonal highs near source; **outburst peaks attenuate downstream while meteorological flood peaks grow** — a clean diagnostic (Cenderelli & Wohl 2001, [Geomorphology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X0100037X)). Chamoli: 8,200–14,200 → 2,900–4,900 m³/s over 15→26 km.
- **Dilution — the direct "river water joins the flow" precedent:** Pierson & Scott 1985 ([WRR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/WR021i010p01511)): 19 Mar 1982 Mt St Helens lahar started as 4 Mm³ of crater water, bulked to debris flow, then over 81 km was **progressively diluted by river water**, transforming to hyperconcentrated flow ~27 km downstream. Debris flow → hyperconcentrated flow → sediment-laden flood is the canonical distal sequence; **by the distal end most of the water is river water.**
- **Junctions & cascading dams re-sharpen peaks:** Melamchi's sequential dams, Sikkim's 385-km cascade (Chungthang dam destroyed at ~65 km; most erosion within ~67.5 km; 45 secondary landslides), Kolka's arrest + distal mudflow, and the reported 2026 Lhende blockage. Cook et al. 2018 ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat4981)): even a modest GLOF dominates erosion tens of km downstream because it moves boulders the monsoon cannot.
- **Net expectation for a 100-km monsoon runout:** solids drop out in the first few tens of km; the surviving wave is increasingly watery, steep-fronted, longer-duration, decaying peak *except* where temporary dams re-sharpen it — and water carried at 50–100 km is overwhelmingly channel-derived.

## 6. Modeling tools

- **r.avaflow v4** (Mergili & Pudasaini; [GMD 2025](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/18/9879/2025/), [avaflow.org](https://www.avaflow.org)): open-source GRASS-GIS multi-phase (solid/fine-solid/fluid) with entrainment. Used for Chamoli (inputs published, reproducible). Best for the upper 10–30 km at 10–20 m cells; calibration-hungry.
- **D-Claw** (Iverson & George; [GitHub](https://github.com/geoflows/D-Claw)): two-phase with real pore-pressure/dilatancy physics — Iverson 2011 wet-bed feedback is native. No thermal/melt physics; steeper learning curve.
- **HEC-RAS 2D** (6.x non-Newtonian options): the right tool for the distal 50–100 km — route candidate hydrographs down a DEM channel, compare arrival times/stages at known points. Overnight on a laptop at 20–50 m mesh.
- **1D Saint-Venant / kinematic routing with source terms:** entirely respectable core — lateral-inflow + entrainment terms directly test "cumulative river water" vs "melt slug at source" against observed timing; transparent water bookkeeping by reach; seconds to run.
- **Data:** Copernicus GLO-30 ([OpenTopography](https://portal.opentopography.org/raster?opentopoID=OTSDEM.032021.4326.3)); HMA 8-m DEM ([NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/highmountainasia)); Sentinel-2; DHM stn 447; hydropower design docs.

## Synthesis

1. **Energetics bound melt.** A ~1,200 m fall cannot melt more than a modest fraction of its ice; Chamoli needed 3,400 m AND a near-perfect 80:20 ratio to melt ~5 Mm³, and its authors called that combination rare.
2. **Precedents where non-melt water dominated:** Seti 2012 (Kargel's own conclusion), Melamchi 2021, Huascarán 1970, every lahar following the Pierson & Scott sequence.
3. **The monsoon Trishuli holds ~10–30 Mm³ standing water per 100 km + ~2 Mm³/h inflow vs a melt ceiling of a few Mm³** — beyond the first few tens of km the wave's water is almost necessarily channel-dominated (displacement, impoundment-release, pore-water liberation, progressive dilution).
4. **The strongest counter-consideration:** Chamoli — best-instrumented analog, Kargel co-author — concluded the opposite *for its own winter, short-runout case*. Any write-up must present that explicitly and show why monsoon season, a 1,200 m fall, and a 100+ km runout flip the balance of terms rather than contradict Shugar et al.

**Carried uncertainties:** Seti peaks 1,000–12,300 m³/s; Huascarán volumes differ 10× across literature; 2016 Bhote Koshi released volume inconsistent (10⁵ vs 10⁸ m³; small figure credible); heat-partition fractions 0.3–0.7 are tuned, not measured.
