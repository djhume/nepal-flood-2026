Subject: Data request — Bhote Koshi/Trishuli flood of 26 August: Devghat and
Galchhi records

Dear Dr Bhattarai and colleagues,

First, my sincere condolences to Nepal and to your Division for the losses of
26 August, including your own stations and the many hydropower workers among
the missing. I have presented professionally in Nepal and the country is close
to my heart.

I am a Principal Advisor (Engineering) in New Zealand's electricity sector.
Independently of my employer, I have been building a physics-based analysis of
the flood — water provenance, wave routing, and warning-time budgets — drawing
on your Division's excellent 27 August press release, which anchors the work.
The analysis (shareable link on request, or attached) reaches preliminary
conclusions that may interest you: most of the flood water appears to be
monsoon river water swept up by the debris wave rather than glacial melt, and
the warning-time budget divides the corridor into a lower reach where SMS
alerting works and an upper reach that only automated triggering can protect.

To test and correct this work, I would be very grateful for:

1. The identity of the **Devghat station** used in your report (station
   number, and whether it gauges the Narayani below the Kali Gandaki
   confluence or the Trishuli arm), with its rating curve and typical late-
   August base flow.
2. The **Devghat stage/discharge time series** for 26 August (your report
   cites 14:10–18:00), at whatever resolution exists.
3. The **Galchhi station record** for 26–27 August — the one telemetered
   station in the flood path that survived, and to my knowledge the most
   valuable single hydrograph of the event.
4. Any partial records from **Betrawati (st. 447), Syabrubesi, Malekhu and
   Kalikhola** before washout, and station 447's historic late-August flow
   statistics.
5. The full **technical report** behind the ~2 crore m³ excess-volume
   estimate, so I can represent your method accurately.

In return I am glad to share all code, data and findings openly, acknowledge
DHM in any output, and contribute — in whatever way is useful — to thinking on
automated upper-reach warning for events of this class.

With respect and thanks,
